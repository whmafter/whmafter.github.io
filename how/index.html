How we calculate

The rules this website and the app use, and how we checked the calculation.

How this website calculates

  • It uses the Saju Bomi app's own calculation code, so the same birthday gives the same result as the app.
  • If you choose your country of birth, it uses that country's time zone; if not, your device's time zone. It doesn't adjust for your birth city's sun time (longitude). Adding your birth city in the app makes it more precise.
  • The detailed tables, the "Why?" behind each paragraph and the birth-time explorer are checked against the app's own screens, too.
  • Without a birth time, it shows only what doesn't depend on the hour, and says so.
  • For fun and entertainment only.
  • We check that this website gives the app's exact results by comparing it with the app's own functions: 12,206 cases in two languages, no differences (2026-11-07).

How this app checks its calculations

Every time the app changes, tests compare its solar-term times and four pillars with separate calculations. This checks that the calculation is right; it doesn't mean the readings come true.

Solar terms (1900–2100)

The app holds the start times of 2,412 solar terms, computed from NASA JPL's DE440s ephemeris. Against a separate astronomical calculation they differ by 0.25 seconds on average and 0.53 seconds at most.

Four pillars

Tests compare them with two other calculation libraries: 5,000 randomly chosen births (lunar-python) and 2,000 births in Korea (manseryeok-js). Cases where a library follows a different rule (within minutes of a solar term, how the zi hour is split, and so on) are marked and left out of that comparison.

Seoul and other cities

1,850 births in Seoul and 8 other cities, each calculated with both zi-hour rules and with longitude correction on and off (7,400 charts), all matched the astronomical calculation (October 2026).

Daylight saving and standard time

Daylight saving and standard time come from the time-zone records (IANA time zone data) for that place and date. The result is shown above under “Time used”.

Same chart, same words

The same person, day and settings always give the same reading. Nothing is picked at random.
