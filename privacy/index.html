Privacy Policy

Saju Bomi · Effective November 7, 2026

Saju Bomi ("the app") is a character-based app about saju, the Korean Four Pillars of Destiny, made by Dion Labs ("we"), the developer name of Hyunmin Woo (우현민), a sole proprietor in the Republic of Korea trading as Dion (디온). This policy explains what information the app handles, where it is kept, and who else receives it. A Korean version with the same content is here.

  • What you enter (names and birth details of you and the people you add) and the readings you save are kept only on your phone. We don't run servers and we never receive them.
  • If your phone's backup is on, your saved people, saved readings, records and general settings can be included in your phone's backup (usually your Google account) and copied to a new phone.
  • If you make a backup file in Settings, this information goes into one file that is not password-protected, and the file goes only where you choose.
  • Google's ad service (AdMob) runs only when you choose to watch an ad. It then collects some device information to show and measure ads.
  • Purchases are handled by Google Play. Google Play's billing library sends Google diagnostic information about your device and its connection.
  • There is no account, and the app has no analytics or tracking tools of our own.
  • On our website, the birth details you enter (for compatibility, the other person's too) are worked out inside your browser and are not sent or saved. Visits to the website are counted with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which does not use cookies; we see only totals.
  • If you join the closed test's tester group on Google Groups, we can see your Google account email address and the name you use in the group (a display name, or your Google profile name and photo).

1. Information you enter

For you and for each person you add, the app stores:

  • a name or nickname,
  • the birth date and its calendar (solar or lunar, and whether it is a leap month),
  • the birth time, if you know it (if you don't, the rough time of day you chose: morning, daytime, evening or night),
  • the birthplace, if you know it: how far you chose (country, region or city), and the time zone and longitude used for the calculation,
  • gender, if you choose one.

The app uses this to work out the saju chart and show the character, daily readings, the lifetime report and compatibility. Charts are calculated on your phone each time and are not sent anywhere. Your answers on whether readings fit and your saved readings do keep the chart or birth details from that time (see the table below). The information is kept in the app's private storage on your phone. If you add someone else's details, please make sure they are happy for you to do so.

3. Phone backup and moving to a new phone

The app allows Android's backup. If backup is turned on for your phone, Android includes the app's saved people, saved readings and the general settings above in the phone's backup (usually stored in your Google account), and copies them when you transfer data to a new phone. The records marked "No" above, notification schedules, ad-related data and temporary images are not included.

Backups are made and stored by your phone and Google under their terms; we can't access them. Your phone backs up on its own, usually about once a day when it is not in use and is on Wi-Fi, so recent changes may not be included, and restoring is not guaranteed. You can turn backup off, or delete backups, in your phone's settings and your Google account.

If you tap “Make a backup file” in Settings, the app puts your saved people (everything listed in section 1, and which person is you), saved readings, your answers on whether readings fit, D-days, your daily log, collected solar term cards and the general settings above into one file and hands it to your phone's share sheet. Before making it, the app tells you what goes into the file. The file is not password-protected, so anyone who has it can open and read it. You choose where the file goes; neither the app nor we send or receive it. Copies you keep elsewhere (such as a drive or a messenger) are handled by those services. The copy the share sheet reads is kept in the app's private temporary storage until your next share. The records marked "No" above (such as purchase records) are not included; on a new phone, restore purchases from Google Play. Restoring the file on another phone replaces the saved people, records and settings on that phone. The file contains personal information, so keep it somewhere only you can reach.

4. Ads (Google AdMob)

The app shows rewarded ads only when you choose to watch one to open readings for later days. Google's ad service starts only at that point; it is not used otherwise.

When it runs, Google's Mobile Ads SDK collects, according to Google's disclosure:

  • your IP address, which may be used to estimate your general location,
  • interactions such as app launches, taps and video views,
  • diagnostic information such as app and SDK performance,
  • device identifiers: the Android advertising ID and app set ID (and, where applicable, identifiers related to accounts signed in on the device).

Google uses this for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, encrypts it in transit, and processes and stores it under its policies, including outside your country. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland and other places where it is required, Google's consent form asks for your choices before ads are requested; you can change them later in the app's Settings under ad privacy. You can reset or delete your advertising ID, or turn off ad personalization, in your phone's settings.

See Google's Privacy Policy and how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

5. Purchases (Google Play)

Payments are processed by Google Play. We don't receive your payment details. The app asks Google Play whether your account owns the purchase (when you open the app, when you buy and when you tap Restore purchase) and keeps the confirmed-purchase record described above on your phone.

When the app connects to Google Play, Google Play's billing library sends Google diagnostic information about the connection and your device, such as your country, device model and manufacturer, Android version, language, mobile network code, network type, time-zone offset and the app's build number (what we saw when testing the app). See Google's Privacy Policy.

Once, after a few days of use, the app may open Google Play's rating window (right after you save a reading or finish sharing an image). The window belongs to Google Play: the stars and any review you write go to Google Play, not to the app, and the app is not told whether you left one. Reviews left during closed testing are shown privately to us instead of publicly.

6. Notifications, character letters, your calendar, listening and the home screen widget

If you turn them on, morning, solar term, character letter, D-day and year-ahead monthly notifications are scheduled on your phone and their text is created on your phone. No server sends them. You can turn them off in the app or in your phone's settings. A D-day notification shows the name you gave the D-day and, for someone else's D-day, that person's saved name. Your phone's settings decide whether notification content shows on the lock screen. Character letters are worked out on your phone from your own birth details; they are not stored or sent anywhere.

You can add D-days, good days, the months to use and watch in your year ahead, and solar terms to your phone calendar. They come from the chart of the person you are viewing, so they may be about someone else. Adding one at a time passes its title and date to your calendar app's new-event screen, and you save it in that app. Adding several at once hands a calendar file (.ics) with those events to your phone's share sheet; the file holds dates, titles (including the names you gave D-days) and a fixed description, but no one's name or birth date. If your calendar app syncs with an account, that service handles the events. The app does not read your calendar.

Listening reads readings aloud with a voice installed on your phone (text-to-speech). The app uses only voices that work without the internet, and hides the listen button if there is none. The text is handled by your phone's speech engine app; the app does not store it or send it to any server.

If you add the home screen widget, the app prepares your character's mood and the day's key line for about the next month on your phone, and the widget shows the one for the day. Because other people can see your home screen, names and birth details are left out, and this data is not included in your phone's backup. Remove the widget from your home screen to stop it.

7. Sharing images, sending text, match invite codes and this website

When you share a card, the image is created on your phone, saved temporarily, passed to the app you pick in the share sheet, and then deleted (the copy the share sheet reads stays in the app's private temporary storage until your next share). Names are included by default and you can leave them out; birth details are off by default and you choose what to include. What you send through other apps is handled by those apps.

"Ask your own AI" turns the calculation and readings for the person you are viewing into text and copies it to your phone's clipboard. Their name, birth date and time, and birthplace are left out by default and you choose what to include. The eight characters of the chart are always in the text, though, and they can be enough to work out the birth date; the ages and years when the ten-year cycles begin give the birth year. The app does not send this text anywhere; the app you paste it into handles it.

"Send my saju as text" turns your own chart and free readings into text that you copy or hand to your phone's share sheet. Your name, birth date and time, and birthplace are all off by default and you choose what to include; the eight characters and the cycle ages that are always included can be enough to work out the birth date. The app does not send this text anywhere; the app you choose handles it.

A match invite code (a link and a QR code) holds only the eight characters of your chart (six if your birth time isn't entered or you leave it out). Your name, birth date and birthplace are not in it, but these characters can be enough to work out the birth date, and eight characters also give a two-hour window for the birth time. You send the code yourself from the share sheet or by copying it; the app does not send it. A code you have sent cannot be taken back, and anyone it is passed on to can open it. When someone opens your code, nothing is sent back to you. In the link, the code comes after the # in the address, so it does not reach the web server. The page the link opens is hosted on GitHub Pages, so GitHub receives the usual access information (IP address and browser details) but not the code. That page does not load the website visit statistics described below. Codes you receive are not saved, and they are gone when you close the screen.

This website (whmafter.github.io): The saju tool (today's fortune, the 2027 New Year fortune, match, your full chart, your love fortune and your health fortune) and the character finder work out readings inside your browser from the birth date you enter, and the birth time and gender if you give them (for compatibility, the other person's as well). These details are not sent to us or anywhere else, are not saved, are not put in the page address, and are gone when you close the page. If you enter someone else's birthday, please check with them first. The character finder's share link holds only the character. The website is hosted on GitHub Pages, so GitHub receives the usual access information (IP address and browser details).

Website visit statistics: To see how many people visit and which pages they view, we use Cloudflare Web Analytics, a service of Cloudflare, Inc. (101 Townsend St., San Francisco, CA 94107, USA). When you open a page on this website, its script sends Cloudflare the address of the page, the page you came from (the referrer) and performance data such as how fast the page loaded. Cloudflare shows us totals by page, referrer, country, browser, operating system and device type. The script does not use cookies or any storage in your browser. Cloudflare receives your IP address with the request, and says it discards it at the nearest Cloudflare data center and does not store it in its core databases or logs. We see only totals that don't identify anyone. Your birth details and results are not in the page address and are not part of what is sent. Cloudflare keeps the full data for 7 days and then a sample of about 10%, and we can view the last 6 months of statistics. The data may be processed in the United States and other countries where Cloudflare operates; see Cloudflare's Privacy Policy. If you block the script (for example with an ad or script blocker), nothing is sent to Cloudflare and the website works the same.

  • We don't sell your information.

10. How long information is kept, and deleting it

Your saved people, saved readings and settings stay on your phone until you delete them in the app or uninstall the app. Deleting a person in the app removes that person, the saved readings that include them, their answers on whether readings fit, their D-days and their daily log from the phone. You can delete D-days and days in your daily log one by one. You can also delete saved readings one by one, and remove an answer by tapping it again or delete all answers and your whole daily log with Delete records in Settings. Uninstalling the app removes all of its data from the phone. Copies in your phone's backup are kept and deleted under your phone's and Google's settings. Backup files you made stay wherever you put them: deleting people or records in the app doesn't change those copies, and restoring such a file brings back what you deleted. Delete files you no longer need from where you keep them. Information collected by Google is kept under Google's policies. Website visit statistics are kept by Cloudflare as described in section 7.

We delete emails to us once the request is handled, except that records of consumer complaints or disputes about purchases are kept separately for 3 years as required by Korea's Act on Consumer Protection in Electronic Commerce, and then deleted.

11. Age

The app is intended for adults aged 18 and over. It is not directed at children or teenagers, and we don't run a separate service for them. The app doesn't ask for your age. The birth dates you enter are used for readings and may belong to other people, so the app doesn't treat them as your age. Information entered in the app stays on that phone, where it can be deleted. The fortune tool on this website doesn't ask your age and anyone can use it. The birth dates entered there are worked out in your browser and are neither stored nor sent, so the tool doesn't collect anyone's personal information from what is entered.

12. Your choices and rights

Because your information is on your phone, you can see, correct and delete it in the app at any time. For information collected by Google (ads, purchases, backups), use Google's controls and your phone's settings. To see or delete emails you sent us, contact us (below). If you have a question or a complaint about privacy, contact us (below).

Users in Korea can also contact the Personal Information Dispute Mediation Committee (1833-6972, kopico.go.kr), the Personal Information Infringement Report Center (118, privacy.kisa.or.kr), the Supreme Prosecutors' Office (1301, spo.go.kr) or the Korean National Police Agency (182, ecrm.police.go.kr).

13. Security

Your information is kept in the app's private storage, which Android protects from other apps, and the app does not send it anywhere. What you share yourself or export as a backup file is wherever you put it, and backup files are not password-protected. Information sent by Google's services is encrypted in transit by Google.
